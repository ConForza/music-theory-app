## 2. A representative lesson from each unit

### G1-U1-L06: Dotted notes

**Objective.** Use a dot to make a note half as long again.

- A dot written after a note makes it longer: it adds half of the note's own value.
- [notation] Dotted minim: 3 beats. Dotted crotchet: 1½ beats. Dotted quaver: ¾ beat.
- A minim is 2 beats, and half of 2 is 1, so a dotted minim is 3 beats. A crotchet is 1 beat and half of 1 is ½, so a dotted crotchet is 1½ beats.
- A dot does the same job as a tie to a note half the size. A dotted minim sounds exactly like a minim tied to a crotchet.
- To find a dotted note's length: take the note's beats, then add half of that.

**Recap.** A dot adds half of the note's own length. | Dotted minim = 3 beats, dotted crotchet = 1½ beats. | A dotted note sounds the same as the note tied to one half its size.

### G1-U2-L04: Grouping notes to show the beat

**Objective.** Group short notes with beams so that the beats can be seen.

- When a bar contains short notes, we join them with beams in groups that show the beat. This makes the music easy to read at a glance.
- [notation] Each pair of quavers is one beat, so each pair has its own beam.
- [notation] The same rhythm with flags only is harder to read. Beam quavers in groups that fill a beat.
- In 4/4, keep the middle of the bar visible: do not run a beam from beat 2 into beat 3. Start a new beam on each beat.
- In 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4 each crotchet beat gets its own group: a pair of quavers, or four semiquavers.

**Recap.** Beam short notes in groups that fill a beat. | Do not run a beam across the middle of a 4/4 bar. | Grouping makes the beats easy to see.

### G1-U3-L04: Treble clef: notes in the spaces

**Objective.** Read and place the four treble-clef space notes (F, A, C, E).

- The treble clef has four spaces. Reading from the bottom, their notes spell a word: F, A, C, E.
- [notation] The space notes from the bottom: F A C E, which spells FACE.
- Lines and spaces take turns, and the letter names climb the alphabet one step each time: E on the bottom line, then F in the first space, then G on the next line.
- [notation] Line notes and space notes together make one climbing alphabet.
- Forgotten a space note? Use the lines either side. The space between the G line and the B line must be A.

**Recap.** The treble-clef space notes from the bottom are F, A, C, E (FACE). | Lines and spaces take turns, climbing the alphabet one step at a time. | Use the neighbouring line notes to check a space note.

### G1-U4-L03: How long does an accidental last?

**Objective.** Know how long a sharp or flat lasts within a bar.

- An accidental changes the note it is written in front of, and it keeps working for any later note on the same line or space in the same bar.
- [notation] The F sharp in beat 3 has no sign of its own, but it is still sharp: the first sharp lasts to the end of the bar.
- The accidental stops at the bar-line. In the next bar the same note goes back to normal, unless a new sign is written.
- [notation] Bar 1 has an F sharp. In bar 2 the F is plain F again.
- The accidental belongs to one line or space only. A sharp on this F does not affect an F in a different octave.
- Ask two questions: 'Is it the same line or space?' and 'Am I still in the same bar?' If both are yes, the accidental still applies.

**Recap.** An accidental lasts to the end of the bar. | It affects only its own line or space. | A new bar starts afresh.

### G1-U5-L03: G and D major: raising notes

**Objective.** Build the scales of G major and D major by raising notes with sharps.

- If we start a major scale on G and use only white keys, the pattern goes wrong: the last step is a tone instead of a semitone. To fix it, the 7th note must be raised to F sharp.
- [notation] G major: G A B C D E F♯ G. One sharp, on the 7th note.
- D major starts on D. To keep the pattern we need two sharps: F sharp and C sharp.
- [notation] D major: D E F♯ G A B C♯ D. Sharps on the 3rd and 7th notes.
- Notice every letter name is used once, in order. We write F♯, never G♭, because the scale would then skip F and use G twice.
- After writing a scale, check the pattern: semitones come between notes 3-4 and 7-8.

**Recap.** G major has one sharp: F♯. | D major has two sharps: F♯ and C♯. | Each letter is used once, so write F♯ and not G♭.

### G1-U6-L01: The tonic triad

**Objective.** Recognise and build the tonic triad of C, G, D and F major.

- A triad is a chord of three notes, built by taking every other note of the scale: degree 1, degree 3 and degree 5. The tonic triad starts on the tonic, degree 1.
- [notation] The tonic triad of C major: C (1), E (3) and G (5).
- When the root, the note the triad is named after, is the lowest note, the triad is in root position. In this course all triads are in root position.
- [notation] The tonic triad of G major: G, B and D. All three are in the key without any change.
- [notation] The tonic triad of D major: D, F♯ and A. The 3rd, F♯, comes from the major scale, so it needs a sharp.
- Take the scale, then every other note: skip a note, take a note, skip a note, take a note.

**Recap.** A triad has three notes: degrees 1, 3 and 5. | The tonic triad is built on degree 1. | In root position the root is the lowest note.

### G1-U7-L05: Looking closely at a melody

**Objective.** Read a short melody and answer questions about what you see and what it means.

- Reading music means using everything you know at once. Work through a melody with a checklist: the clef, the key signature, the time signature, the tempo word, the dynamic marks, and then the notes, rests and signs bar by bar.
- [notation] An original melody: G major, 4/4, Andante, mf. Bar 1 has a staccato note marked x, bar 3 ends with a rest, and bar 4 has a pause.
- Here the key signature has one sharp, so the key is G major and the tonic is G. The time signature 4/4 means four crotchet beats in each bar. Andante tells you to go at a walking pace, and mf means moderately loud.
- The note marked x is B. It is a 3rd above the tonic G, because G A B counts as three. It has a dot, so it is played staccato: short and detached.
- Take it one bar at a time. If a question asks about 'the highest note', look at every bar before you answer.

**Recap.** Check the clef, key signature, time signature and tempo word first. | Read the marks on the notes: dynamics, staccato, accents, slurs and pauses. | Name notes and intervals above the tonic using the key.

## 4. Notation, grouping and rest conventions the rule set enforces (please confirm each is Grade 1)

- Beam short notes in groups that fill one beat; do not beam across the middle of a 4/4 bar.
- Give each beat its own rest; in 4/4 a minim rest may fill beats 1-2 or beats 3-4; a minim rest across beats 2-3 is rejected. In 3/4 a minim rest is currently rejected (EXERCISE_ENGINE section 10).
- Do not tie two notes inside a bar when one note could be written (unnecessary tie). Ties across a bar-line are allowed.
- An accidental lasts to the end of the bar, on its own line or space only; a tied note keeps its accidental across the bar-line.
- Scale spelling: each letter name once, in order; B flat, never A sharp; enharmonic substitutions are rejected where spelling is assessed.
- Intervals are named by number only (the lower note is always the tonic); triads are root-position tonic triads only.
- Stem direction is mentioned in G1-U3-L07 for neatness but never assessed.

**Compared with the reference book's own Grade 1 list** (read for verification only): the book also lists _poco_ (a little), _dal segno_, _ritenuto_, _8va_ and metronome marks, and draws the crescendo / diminuendo hairpins; this course keeps _dal segno_ and _ritenuto_ at Grade 2, leaves _poco_, _8va_ and metronome marks out, and teaches crescendo / diminuendo as words. This course also includes _dolce_, which is not in the book's Grade 1 list. The 2020 outline does not enumerate terms, so a teacher decision is needed (Q2).
