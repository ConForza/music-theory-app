### The Grade 4 assessment sample

41 questions a run: **2 for each of the 18 Grade 4 outcomes (36)** and **5 earlier-grade questions** (Grade 3 templates and melodies from Grades 1 and 2, so the mixed run still revisits what Grade 4 builds on). The pass mark (66%, unchanged) needs 28 right answers, so at least 23 must be Grade 4 answers: a learner cannot pass by showing Grade 1-3 knowledge, and a test asserts it. The existing planned sample (36 + 5) was reviewed for fairness and **retained**: every outcome is asked twice in every run (tested for six runs per test pass), each outcome section draws from every template that can honestly evidence it, and the earlier-grade section can use only Grade 1-3 templates. Outcomes that rest on several concepts (g4.01 metre and grouping; g4.05 alto reading and alto key signatures; g4.10 sharp and flat keys; g4.13 plain and augmented/diminished intervals; g4.18 orchestra, techniques and passages) are sampled, not exhaustively covered, in one run; the unit challenges and the practice and review nodes cover the rest. Section ids are unique and the question count of every blueprint is asserted exactly by the authoring script and by a test.

**No false mastery from earlier grades.** A concept boundary (`content/policy/review-constraints.json`) decides which questions credit a Grade 4 concept, whatever template asked them: regular-time-signature concepts credit only the eight signatures that are new at Grade 4; the breve and double-dot concepts credit only questions about those values; alto-clef and alto key-signature concepts credit only questions that name the alto clef; the sharp/flat-key concepts credit only B major, G♯ minor, D♭ major and B♭ minor; the interval concepts split major/minor/perfect from augmented/diminished by the answer; the primary-triad concept ignores a tonic-only chord; the terms concept credits only the 21 Grade 4 terms (not the Grade 1-3 term list); the techniques concept only the eight techniques. Tests in `grade4-course.test.ts` pin each boundary.

**Honest limits of the counts.** Distinct means a different question identity (template, prompt, music, answer; option order is ignored). Some concepts rest on few underlying facts (the breve has one length and one rest; two sharp and two flat keys; eight techniques; 21 terms), so their variety comes from several question types rather than from many different facts.

## 7. Answer checks

The generators write their own answers and the engine's solvability gate grades those same answers, which is circular for choice questions. `packages/engine/test/grade4-answers.test.ts` therefore re-derives each Grade 4 answer from what the learner SEES (notation read back with the curriculum's own accidental rules, key signature and bar state applied) and from the prompt, with the rule functions (`checkBarAnyPattern`, `scale`, `intervalBetween`, `identifyTriad`, `checkChromatic`, `resolveWrittenPitches`) and compares:

- breves and double dots: lengths by arithmetic on `dotted()`; the accepted bar fills the bar exactly; the breve rest and tied-note answers are single, unique answers; double-dotted rests are asked only as lengths;
- time signatures: kind, beats, beat note and bar length from the signature; for every rhythm asked, exactly one of the eighteen regular signatures fits every bar; grouping judgements run every bar through the bar checker (the right bar is clean, each wrong bar breaks only a grouping rule, every bar adds up);
- duplets: durations from the tuplet ratio; every bar of the reading questions adds up;
- double accidentals, enharmonics, the alto clef: the printed note is read back (signs and bar state) and compared with the accepted pitch; same sound means the same key; positions of the same pitch in another clef and octave moves are derived from `staffPosition`; the treble and bass readings of an alto position are always offered as wrong options; the same pitch unmoved is always a wrong octave-transposition option;
- keys, degrees, chromatic scales: counts, relatives and raised leading notes from `keyFifths`, `relativeKey` and `scale`; for chromatic judging, exactly one of the four written scales passes the chromatic-scale rule;
- intervals: quality and number from `intervalBetween` on the two printed notes, both notes in the named scale form (harmonic, melodic up, melodic down), the book's quality-change rules checked against real pitches;
- chords: the printed chord (both staves) is read back and identified with `identifyTriad`; the accepted function equals the derived function; the number of notes asked for in chord writing is enforced by the grader;
- ornaments, instruments, terms: descriptions match names; the instrument facts match the reference book's lists; no option comes from the Grade 1-3 vocabulary and no two options share a meaning;
- passages: each part is re-derived from the printed notes, the clef, the printed direction and the instrument named.

Adversarial answers are graded by the real engine in the same file: a note that matches only in sound is not a correct spelling; a rest is refused where a note is needed; the right chord notes with the wrong number of notes are refused; G natural for F double sharp is refused; the same pitch in the new clef, and an octave the wrong way, are refused for octave transposition. Every generated Grade 4 item also accepts its canonical answer and refuses the engine's known wrong answer, and generation is deterministic.

## 8. Known limitations

- No qualified teacher has reviewed any of this; every template, lesson and term list is a draft.
- Teaching quality is untested with real beginners. Sound is synthesised only.
- The Grade 4 term list (21 terms) is the list the reference book says belongs to Grade 4. The official outline publishes no list (Q2), so a teacher must confirm it. Terms with nearly the same meaning (calando, morendo, perdendosi) are never alternatives to one another.
- Double-dotted rests: the book calls them avoidable and the convention for writing them was not verified, so the course asks only their length, never writes one into a bar, and never asks whether one is acceptable.
- Partly silent beats and rests in the new compound times (/4 and /16) are not asked at all: no verified convention exists for them (Brief 07 handoff); only the breve rest is written.
- 2/8 grouping: the book states the grouping for 4/8 only; 2/8 follows the one-beat-one-group idea (to be confirmed). 4/8 and 2/4 cannot be told apart from a rhythm, so 4/8 is never used in 'add the time signature' questions.
- Cancelling double accidentals (Q9): only the natural sign is asked; the older natural-plus-sharp way of reducing a double sharp to a single one is mentioned once and never asked.
- Alto-clef key signatures are read and written (Q13); scales in the alto clef are not asked.
- Intervals in minor keys name the scale form in the prompt (Q27); the 6th and 7th above a minor tonic may therefore now appear, always with the form named.
- Scales are written with accidentals, never with a key signature (Q28); the explanation covers how key signatures and accidentals relate.
- Instrument clefs are described as the clefs instruments 'normally' use: the book underlines the clefs an instrument always uses, and the underlining could not be read reliably from the scan. Percussion clefs are not asked. Transposing instruments are Grade 5 and never asked.
- The bowing signs (down-bow, up-bow) are written as characters and described in words; their appearance depends on the learner's font.
- The chromatic-scale rules accept any spelling that uses each letter once or twice with semitone steps and no double accidentals; the book's preference for the notes of a key signature is mentioned but not examined.
- Chord inversions are Grade 5: chords not in root position are only ever called 'not in root position' and only the root and the position (yes or no) are asked.
- In minor keys the chord prompts write the minor chords with small letters (i, iv) and the dominant V, as the book's extended roman numerals do; learners may answer with capital letters only.
- The renderer draws one form of the breve, and octave signs are not used at Grade 4.
- The pass marks and mastery thresholds are the provisional values from Briefs 04 and 05 (not changed).
