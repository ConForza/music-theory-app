### The Grade 5 assessment sample

39 questions a run: **2 for each of the 16 Grade 5 outcomes (32)** and **7 earlier-grade questions** (Grade 4 templates). The 66% pass mark is compared as an exact ratio (as in Grade 4): 39 questions need **26** right (25 is 64.1%, 26 is 66.7%), so at least 19 must be Grade 5 answers and the seven earlier-grade questions alone can never pass. In the final assessment the constructed skills (transposition, scales, intervals, chords, cadences) are asked by construction or in a real passage, never by facts alone; the unit challenges also ask the fact-style questions. The planned unit challenge sizes were 4, 6, 8, 6 and 12 questions; Unit 1 (two outcomes) is raised to **6** so that every outcome of a challenge has three questions behind it and a pass needs five of six rather than three of four; the others are kept. Every section of every blueprint has distinct ids, and the authoring script asserts the exact counts and that no optional template appears in any blueprint.

**No false mastery from earlier grades or from the wrong kind of question.** A concept boundary (`content/policy/review-constraints.json`, fields added in `question-key.ts`) decides which questions credit a Grade 5 concept, whatever template asked them: chromatic-interval credit needs two notes that share no major or minor scale (a plain C to G earns none); compound-interval credit needs an interval over an octave; inversion credit needs a first or second inversion (a root-position chord earns none); the supertonic concept needs chord ii; four-clef credit needs an alto or tenor clef; octave-transposition credit always involves the tenor clef; six-sharp and six-flat credit goes by the key, never across and never for a Grade 4 key; Grade 5 term credit needs a Grade 5 term (the Grade 4 and Grade 5 term lists share no entry, and a Grade 4 template carries only Grade 4 concepts). Cadence concepts are only on cadence templates, so a basic chord identification never proves cadence understanding. `grade5-course.test.ts` checks each boundary on generated questions and on hand-made counter-examples.

**Honest limits of the counts.** Distinct means a different question identity (template, prompt, music, answer; option order is ignored). Some Grade 5 concepts rest on few underlying facts (the four six-accidental scales have a small number of forms; the ornament descriptions are ten facts), so their many distinct questions are mostly different keys, notes and wrong options around the same facts. Mastery thresholds count independent, first-attempt, hint-free answers, never lesson guided steps and never the optional lessons.

## 7. Answer checks

The generators write their own answers and the engine's solvability gate grades those same answers, which is circular. `packages/engine/test/grade5-answers.test.ts` therefore re-derives each Grade 5 answer from what the learner sees, with small hand-written tables and arithmetic that do not use the production helpers (letter steps and semitone counts for intervals and transposition; the circle of fifths for key signatures; a stack-of-thirds search for chords; fixed tables for the eight forms of the four six-accidental scales), over 100 to 300 seeds per template:

- irregular time: every bar adds up to its signature, the signature is the only one of the four that fits, a stated grouping is judged against that grouping alone (the same total in another grouping is refused), and every irregular group takes exactly the time its ratio says;
- clefs: tenor-clef names counted from D on the bottom line (middle C on the fourth line); the relation of a copy (same pitch, one octave, or merely the stave positions copied) from the actual pitches; octave transposition by letter, accidental and octave number; copied positions graded wrong;
- six-accidental keys: every scale answer equals a hand-typed table (F♯ and G♭ major, D♯ and E♭ minor harmonic and melodic, ascending and descending, with E♯, C𝄪, C♭ and B♯ where they belong); signature counts by the circle of fifths;
- intervals: quality and number from letters and semitones, never doubly augmented or diminished; written notes above and below; compound numbers are the simple number plus seven, and both accepted names agree; respelt, wrong-accidental and wrong-octave notes are graded wrong;
- transposing instruments: every note moved by exactly the stated interval in the stated direction (B♭: a tone, A: a minor 3rd, F: a perfect 5th); the new key found by fifths; the untransposed notes, the wrong direction and the right notes with the old key signature are each graded wrong;
- chords: the root by a stack of thirds and the position by the lowest note agree with the accepted answer; spacing and doubling never change it; the grader gives each of a, b, c the right verdict; chord ii is diminished in minor keys (minor 3rd, diminished 5th);
- cadences: derived from the printed chords of both bars; two cadence points are each named from their own pair; at a cadence point every accepted chord holds its melody note and the melody leaves only the named cadence type possible;
- ornaments: the sign for a written-out figure from letter steps around the main note; no question asks for an ornament to be written out from its sign; voice questions use only the two unmistakable ranges; term questions never offer a synonym of the right answer and never a Grade 4 term;
- passages: several connected parts (instrument, cadence, key, chord, ornament, term, interval) answered right when answered right.

Adversarial answers are graded by the real engine in the same file and in `grade5-course.test.ts`, which also runs the real service: 25 of 39 fails and 26 of 39 passes the Grade 5 assessment (with completion XP and the badge only on the pass), 21 of 32 still passes and 20 of 32 fails the Grade 3 assessment, 27 of 41 fails and 28 of 41 passes the Grade 4 assessment, and a learner who answers only the seven earlier-grade questions cannot pass.

**Browser.** `packages/web/e2e/g5.mjs` plays Grade 5 in a real browser on five viewports (iPhone, 320px, two iPad orientations, desktop), keyboard-only, dark mode, reduced motion and 200% text, answers every kind of question with the real controls (stave, chord, transposition, tuplet, passage) on a 320px phone, checks the spoken descriptions (tenor clef, double sharps, quintuplet and other irregular groups, ornaments by their look) and the exact frequencies of the notes played for the transposition demonstrations (a stubbed audio context records the real oscillator frequencies).

## 8. Known limitations

- No qualified teacher has reviewed any of this; every template, lesson and term, instrument and voice list is a draft.
- Teaching quality is untested with real beginners. Sound is synthesised only.
- The Grade 5 term list (the Italian, German and direction lists of the reference book) is the one the book gives; the official outline publishes none (Q2). Terms with nearly the same meaning are never offered as wrong answers for each other. Grade 4 terms are not asked as Grade 5 terms.
- Voice ranges (Q34): the book gives none. The ranges in the vocabulary are provisional teaching values; only the two unmistakable extremes are asked (wholly above the alto's D5 is soprano, wholly below the tenor's C3 is bass).
- Ornaments: how each sign is performed is taught and asked only as a description. No question asks for the notes of an ornament to be written from its sign (the outline allows only replacing written-out notes with signs), and the inverted turn and the appoggiatura/acciaccatura cannot be told from a written-out figure, so they are never asked in the replacement task.
- Irregular time (Q30, Q35): the grouping in x/4 is shown by note lengths because crotchets cannot be beamed; beamed grouping is read in x/8 only; a rest fills a whole group or a single unit, never a part of a group. These conventions are not verified by a teacher.
- Key signatures in the alto and tenor clefs (Q13) are read and written with the clef named in the prompt; scales are asked in the treble and bass clefs only, as the book says.
- Chord identification and writing (Q14) are both asked. Roman numerals follow the book's two accepted ways (letters a, b, c or figures) and either capital-only or lower-case numerals (Q33); the diminished sign is explained, not required. Chord writing is asked in the treble and the bass clef: the question shows an empty stave in the asked clef with the key signature, and the learner writes any spacing and any doubling that has the right notes and the right lowest note.
- Cadences: asked only in C, G, D and F major, only where both cadence chords are in root position, and a melody question accepts every pair of chords that contains the melody notes and makes the named cadence. The validator derives the possible cadence types from the melody, so a melody question is only generated when the melody allows exactly the named type.
- Transposing instruments (Q36): the direction and the interval are always stated, and the question says whether the given music is written or concert pitch. The instrument's name is offered as a wrong answer for the key of a transposed part. Performance directions are not carried into the answer (they cannot be graded).
- Chromatic intervals (Q32/Q27): never doubly augmented or diminished, as the book says; intervals between notes of one scale in a minor key follow the Grade 4 convention of naming the form where it matters.
- Optional enrichment (Q8): the Ic–V progression, approach chords and SATB in short and open score are playable but never compulsory; transcription between short and open score is not assessed (the outline does not require it). In the approach-chord lesson the grader accepts any of I, ii, IV or V that holds the melody note for the approach chord (it does not check that the approach chord differs from the next chord).
- The renderer announces irregular groups by size (quintuplet, sextuplet, septuplet, nonuplet) in its spoken names; the octave sign for open-score tenor parts is not drawn, so it is described in words only.
- The pass marks and mastery thresholds are the provisional values from Briefs 04 and 05 (not changed); Grade 5 uses the exact-ratio comparison of Grade 4, Grades 1-3 keep the released rounded rule.
