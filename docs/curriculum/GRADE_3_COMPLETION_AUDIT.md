## 7. Answer checks

The generators write their own answers and the engine's solvability gate grades those same answers, which is circular for choice questions. `packages/engine/test/grade3-answers.test.ts` therefore re-derives each new Grade 3 answer from what the learner is shown (the printed notation and the prompt) with the curriculum rule functions, and compares:

- compound facts: from the printed time signature (top number divided by three, beat note, kind of time, quavers per bar) or from the beat described;
- bar judging (notes and rests): every one of the three bars is run through the bar-rule checker; exactly the chosen bar is free of issues, the others break a grouping or a rest rule, all three add up, and a 12/8 half-bar rest is never offered as the wrong answer;
- rest writing: the accepted bar is clean; the crotchet-plus-quaver form, the dotted-crotchet form and (in 12/8) the half-bar form are accepted by the real grader; three quaver rests and two silent 9/8 beats joined are refused;
- triplet and compound comparisons: partner signatures, note counts and lengths from the printed bar, and (for the older time-signature template) every signature in scope that fits the printed beaming must equal the accepted set;
- demisemiquaver questions: flags, beams, halves and bar totals by arithmetic; beaming by the bar checker; never more than four demisemiquavers in a row inside a beat;
- ledger lines: the named position gives exactly one pitch with at least two (placing) or three (counting, reading) ledger lines;
- octave signs and octave transposition: direction from the printed sign, octave from the pitch; the same pitch in the other clef is always a wrong option and a clef change without the octave move is refused by the real grader;
- minor forms and scale gaps: from `scale(key, form)` in playing order; the printed notes belong to exactly one of the four forms/directions offered;
- intervals: from `intervalBetween` on the two printed notes, from the scale for major/minor facts, and from semitone counts;
- terms: from the vocabulary table, no near-synonym or second speed word beside a speed target; melodies: from the printed bars, key signature and marked note.

Hand-typed anchors in the same file: the notes of A, F♯, C♯, C, F and G minor in the forms taught, E major, A♭ major, and the 12/8 half-bar rest rule.

The wider course tests (`grade3-course.test.ts`) check that every question in every practice, review and challenge run lies inside the taught scope of its node and credits at least one concept, that no node shows an idea from a later unit, that the grade assessment covers all twelve outcomes (with melodic-minor and compound-time questions in every run), that failed challenges and assessments pay nothing and can be retaken with both results recorded, that the optional lesson never gates or counts, and that no lesson repeats a question.

## 8. Known limitations

- No qualified teacher has reviewed any of this; every template, lesson and term list is a draft.
- Teaching quality is untested with real beginners. Sound is synthesised only.
- The Grade 3 term list is the provisional vocabulary (Q2): only six terms are first used at Grade 3 (andantino, prestissimo, tempo primo, espressivo, sostenuto, con moto). The reference book lists many more Grade 3 words; they are not added because the vocabulary is part of every generated question's fingerprint, and changing it would make stored Grade 1-2 question ids stale. The meaning of andantino varies between sources.
- Octave signs are drawn as text (8va above the first bar, 8vb under the first note), not as an engraved dashed bracket; the sign is always named in the question and its text description.
- Compound-time rests follow the provisional policy (Q4): a silent dotted-crotchet beat is one dotted-crotchet rest or a crotchet rest plus a quaver rest, both accepted. Partly silent beats (a note then a rest inside one beat) are never asked because their convention was not verified. The half-bar dotted-minim rest of 12/8 (beats 1-2 or 3-4) was added to the rule checker (Q25); the checker's note rules for 12/8 were not changed.
- Beats never hold more than four demisemiquavers in a row, because the rule checker asks for one beam over a whole beat while the reference book also allows two groups of four (Q26).
- The ledger-line limit is four (Q6), a provisional teacher decision. An introduction row in scope-concepts.json lets the scope reach four lines.
- The optional upbeat lesson (Q5) has fixed questions only, no generator, and is never assessed, reviewed or counted for mastery. Context melodies never use an upbeat.
- In minor keys the 6th and 7th above the tonic are never asked as an interval by number alone (the form would be ambiguous); they appear only as intervals between two printed notes or to build.
- The 4/4 versus 2/2 style ambiguity is avoided in Grade 3 time-signature questions by checking, for every generated rhythm, which of the signatures in scope fit its beaming (an engine test pins this); the older Grade 3 template is otherwise unchanged.
- Three Brief 01 Grade 3 templates gained scope dimensions (rests, note values, ties) so lesson bindings can hold back untaught material; the planned Grade 4 review bindings were updated to state them. No Grade 1-2 template or stored question identity changed.
- Scales are written with accidentals, never with a key signature (Q28). The repository's outcome wording does not say which is required, the official syllabus could not be inspected, and Grades 1-2 ask the same way; the reference book shows both. The assessment requirement was not changed; a teacher decides.
- The demisemiquaver rest is recognised and its length compared with other rests (four make a quaver rest); writing it inside a beat is not asked because the arrangement convention was not verified (Q4, Q26).
- The pass marks and mastery thresholds are the provisional values from Briefs 04 and 05 (not changed).
